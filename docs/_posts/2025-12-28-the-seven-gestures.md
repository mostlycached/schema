When you're working on something and feel stuck, it's rarely because you lack ideas. It's because the position you're in has blocked the movements you need.

Every creative position — what we call a *site* — has embedded dynamics: certain movements are available, others are blocked, some feel natural. These movements are **gestures**.

There are seven of them.

---

## Gestures Are Possibility Operations

A gesture isn't a metaphor or a theme. It's an *operation on possibility space*.

When you BRANCH, you create multiple divergent paths without committing to any. The space of what-could-happen expands.

When you NARROW, you add a constraint that reduces the space. Fewer things are possible, but those things are more specific.

These are movements. You can execute them. They have consequences.

---

## The Seven Gestures

### BRANCH
**Direction**: Opening  
**What it does**: Creates divergent paths without committing

You're at a site. BRANCH generates 2-5 new sites, each a different direction the work could go. You haven't chosen; you've multiplied options.

*In writing*: "The story could go this way, or this way, or this way."  
*In mathematics*: "Consider the following cases..."  
*In music*: "Theme and variations."

**Consequences**:
- Increases possibility space
- Creates multiple history branches
- Reversible (you can return and take a different branch)
- Not time-sensitive (branches stay open)

---

### NARROW
**Direction**: Closing  
**What it does**: Reduces possibilities by adding constraints

You add a constraint: "The character is lying." Now many sites that were available become invisible, and sites involving deception become vivid.

*In writing*: "Specifying that she's unreliable changes everything."  
*In mathematics*: "Adding the hypothesis that x > 0."  
*In music*: "Confining ourselves to the dorian mode."

**Consequences**:
- Decreases possibility space
- History continues linearly (but constrained)
- Reversible (you can loosen constraints later via WIDEN)
- Not time-sensitive

NARROW is not COMMIT. Narrowing adds constraints; committing forecloses alternatives. You can undo a narrowing. You can't undo a commitment.

---

### COMMIT
**Direction**: Closing  
**What it does**: Forecloses alternatives irrevocably

The character dies. The theorem is published. The note is played. There's no going back.

*In writing*: "Irreversible plot events, revelations that can't be unknown."  
*In mathematics*: "Assert lemma, take as proven, build on it."  
*In music*: "The cadence that ends the phrase."

**Consequences**:
- Collapses possibility space
- Closes branches (they're now roads not taken, forever)
- **Irreversible**
- Blocks BRANCH and DEFER at the committed point

COMMIT is the gesture that makes work *actual*. Without it, everything stays in superposition. But it forecloses — that's its nature. The unused branches become ghosts.

---

### DEFER
**Direction**: Holding  
**What it does**: Preserves options by postponing commitment

You don't reveal who sent the letter. You don't decide which interpretation is correct. You keep the options alive by not choosing.

*In writing*: "Withholding information the reader wants."  
*In mathematics*: "Left as an exercise to the reader."  
*In music*: "Deceptive cadence; not ending when you could."

**Consequences**:
- Neither opens nor closes — holds
- Preserves "option value" on the current site
- Reversible
- **Time-sensitive**: The longer you defer, the more pressure builds. Option value can decay. A deferred decision becomes a source of tension.

DEFER is not passivity. It's an active choice to not-yet-choose, preserving the value of the unchosen.

---

### SUSPEND
**Direction**: Opening  
**What it does**: Holds a question open for later resolution

Different from DEFER. DEFER holds off a decision. SUSPEND opens a question that demands an answer eventually.

You pose "What really happened that night?" and leave it unresolved. The question is now *active* in the work. It creates tension. It will need to be RESOLVED.

*In writing*: "The mystery at the heart of the story."  
*In mathematics*: "An open conjecture we'll return to."  
*In music*: "A suspended chord; a pedal point maintaining tension."

**Consequences**:
- Opens (a question, not branches)
- **Creates obligation**: You've promised resolution eventually
- Time-sensitive: Unresolved suspensions build pressure
- Reversible via RESOLVE

SUSPEND creates the experience of tension—something is held open that presses toward closure.

---

### WIDEN
**Direction**: Opening  
**What it does**: Expands possibility space beyond current boundaries

You've been working within constraints. Now you loosen them, or add new elements, or expand scope.

*In writing*: "Suddenly there's a whole new faction we didn't know about."  
*In mathematics*: "Generalizing the theorem; removing a hypothesis."  
*In music*: "Modulating to a distant key; introducing a new theme."

**Consequences**:
- Increases possibility space (counterpart to NARROW)
- Expands current branch (doesn't create new branches like BRANCH)
- Reversible (can NARROW again)
- Not time-sensitive

WIDEN is not BRANCH. BRANCH creates multiple parallel possibilities. WIDEN makes the current path bigger — more can happen here.

---

### RESOLVE
**Direction**: Closing  
**What it does**: Collapses suspended possibilities into determinate state

You answer the question. The mystery is solved. The tension is discharged.

*In writing*: "The reveal that explains everything."  
*In mathematics*: "Q.E.D."  
*In music*: "Authentic cadence returning to tonic."

**Consequences**:
- Closes (definitively)
- **Discharges obligation** from prior SUSPEND
- **Commits** — resolving is a form of committing
- Irreversible

RESOLVE pairs with SUSPEND. Every SUSPEND creates pressure toward RESOLVE. The work lives in the tension between them.

## The Gesture Compass

The gestures organize into patterns:

| | Opening | Holding | Closing |
|---|---------|---------|---------|
| **Reversible** | BRANCH, WIDEN | DEFER | NARROW |
| **Irreversible** | | | COMMIT, RESOLVE |
| **Time-sensitive** | | DEFER, SUSPEND | |
| **Creates obligation** | SUSPEND | | |
| **Discharges obligation** | | | RESOLVE |

Another way to see it — **three pairs plus one**:

- **BRANCH / NARROW**: Multiply vs. constrain paths
- **SUSPEND / RESOLVE**: Open vs. close questions
- **WIDEN / COMMIT**: Expand scope vs. foreclose alternatives
- **DEFER**: Hold in place (the singular gesture, without pair)

---

## Gestures at Sites

Every site has a gesture profile:

- **Available gestures**: What movements this site affords
- **Dominant gesture**: The movement that feels most natural here
- **Blocked gestures**: What movements are foreclosed
- **Latent gestures**: Movements that are possible but not obvious

Consider the site "the pause in speech":
- **Available**: BRANCH (the pause could go many directions), RESOLVE (the pause ends)
- **Dominant**: SUSPEND (the pause holds open what comes next)
- **Blocked**: COMMIT (a pause can't commit; it's inherently uncommitted)
- **Latent**: WIDEN (the pause could expand, become pregnant silence)

Consider the site "the unlived life":
- **Available**: WIDEN (more unlived possibilities), NARROW (grieving specific paths)
- **Dominant**: DEFER (perpetually postponed)
- **Blocked**: RESOLVE (can never be fully resolved), COMMIT (can't commit to what wasn't lived)
- **Latent**: BRANCH (the unlived life as generative source for imagination)

When you're at a site, you feel which gestures are available. The work often involves following what the site suggests.

---

## Following Gestures

Gestures aren't just for analysis. They're executable.

You're at a site. You BRANCH. The gesture generates new sites — 2-5 divergent possibilities, all stemming from where you were. You're now facing a choice of paths.

You're at a site with accumulated tension. You RESOLVE. The gesture generates a new site — one where the question is answered, the tension discharged. The obligation created by previous SUSPENDS is fulfilled.

You're at a site that feels cramped. You WIDEN. The gesture loosens constraints, expands what can happen. More sites become visible.

This is how you navigate: by executing gestures and arriving at new sites.

---

## Gestural Constraints

You can constrain generation by gesture:

- "Generate a site whose dominant gesture is SUSPEND" → tension-holding sites
- "Generate a site that blocks COMMIT" → sites of perpetual irresolution
- "Generate a site with at least 4 available gestures" → possibility-rich sites
- "Generate a site with only closing gestures" → sites of finality

You can also constrain by gesture **direction**:

- "Primarily opening" → sites where BRANCH, WIDEN, SUSPEND dominate
- "Primarily closing" → sites where NARROW, COMMIT, RESOLVE dominate

This lets you shape what you're looking for before you find it.

---

## Why Gestures Matter

Understanding gestures changes how you work:

**Diagnosis**: When stuck, ask: *What gestures are blocked here?* Maybe you need to WIDEN to break out of constraints. Maybe you need to RESOLVE something that's been SUSPENDED too long. Maybe you need to COMMIT instead of perpetually DEFERRING.

**Navigation**: Instead of asking "What happens next?" ask: *What gesture does this site suggest?* Follow the suggestion. Let the site tell you what movement is available.

**Composition**: Notice your gesture patterns. Do you always BRANCH and never COMMIT? (Generative but never finishing.) Do you always NARROW and never WIDEN? (Focused but cramped.) The balance of gestures shapes the rhythm of work.

**Release**: Sometimes the right move is to WIDEN dramatically — remove constraints you've been treating as fixed. Other times it's to finally COMMIT — stop deferring, accept the foreclosure, and see what becomes possible beyond it.

---

The gestures are the vocabulary of possibility. Learn them, and you learn how creative movement works.
