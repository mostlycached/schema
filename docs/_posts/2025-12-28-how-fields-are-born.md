Before there are sites to explore, there must be a field to explore them in. But where do fields come from?

Fields aren't natural kinds waiting to be discovered. They're *carved* from the flux of experience. The carving is itself a creative act.

---

## What Is a Field?

A field is a bounded, differentiated region of reality that can be attended to.

"Doing laundry" is a field. So is "Renaissance painting." So is "the concept of justice."

Before anyone attended to laundry *as such*, it was just stuff happening — water, fabric, movement. Someone had to *constitute* it as a field: bound it (this, not that), differentiate it (sorting vs. washing vs. drying), make it something that could be attended to.

---

## The Two Operations: Bounding and Differentiating

A field comes into being through two fundamental operations:

### Bounding

Drawing a line around something. "This, not that."

Boundaries can be:
- **Spatial**: Everything in this room
- **Temporal**: The hour spent doing laundry
- **Conceptual**: Everything related to cleanliness
- **Phenomenal**: Everything that feels this way
- **Social**: What this community calls laundry

Bounding without differentiation gives you an empty container. You've drawn a circle, but there's nothing to attend to inside.

### Differentiating

Recognizing internal structure. Finding the joints.

Differentiation can be:
- **Categorical**: Distinct types (whites, colors, delicates)
- **Continuous**: Gradients (more dirty to less dirty)
- **Hierarchical**: Nested levels (laundry contains sorting contains color-sorting)
- **Networked**: Interconnections (sorting affects washing affects drying)

Differentiation without bounding gives you structure you can't locate. You've found joints, but you don't know what they belong to.

Field constitution requires both.

## Five Ways Fields Are Born

Fields don't just appear. They're generated through specific modes:

### 1. Carving Mode

Start with undifferentiated flux. Deliberately bound and differentiate.

You're attending to your morning. Just stuff happening. You try bounding: "the period between waking and leaving." You differentiate: hygiene, dressing, eating, preparing. A field emerges: "morning routine."

### 2. Splitting Mode

Start with an existing field. Split it along fault lines.

"Writing" is a field. You notice fiction and non-fiction operate differently. You split: "fiction writing" and "non-fiction writing" become separate fields. Split again: "short fiction," "novel writing," "flash fiction."

### 3. Merging Mode

Start with multiple fields. Merge them into something new.

"Conversation analysis" and "narrative structure" are separate. You notice both involve sequence and turn-taking. You merge: "sequential structure in discourse" becomes a new field containing both.

### 4. Analogical Mode

Abstract the structure of one field. Apply it to another region.

"Ecosystem" is a field in biology: bounded region, populations, resource flows, predation. You apply this structure to office dynamics. A new field emerges: "organizational ecology."

### 5. Emergence Mode

Don't start anywhere. Let a field emerge from practice.

You're doing phenomenological practice without a clear target. You keep noticing transitions — between tasks, states, contexts. "Transition" starts to cohere as a field. You didn't carve it; it emerged from what your practice kept finding.

## Field Gestures

Like sites, fields have embedded dynamics — operations they afford for their own transformation:

| Gesture | What It Does | Example |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| **EXPAND** | Move boundary outward | "Laundry" → "textile care" |
| **CONTRACT** | Move boundary inward | "Writing" → "sentence-craft" |
| **DEEPEN** | Add levels of differentiation | "Color" → hue, saturation, value, temperature |
| **FLATTEN** | Remove levels of differentiation | "Music theory" → "sound patterns" |
| **BRIDGE** | Connect to distant field | "Cooking" ↔ "chemistry" |
| **ISOLATE** | Disconnect from neighbors | "Pure math" severs from "applied math" |
| **NEST** | Become container for sub-fields | "Art" nests "visual," "performing," "literary" |
| **EMBED** | Become contained by larger field | "Pottery" embeds within "craft" |

When you're working in a field that feels cramped, you might EXPAND. When it feels diffuse, you might CONTRACT. The gestures suggest next moves.

## The Power of Problematization

One way to constitute a field is through a *question*.

"What is justice?" constitutes justice as a field. Before the question, justice was just stuff that happened. The question *bounds* (what falls under justice?) and *differentiates* (what kinds of justice? what aspects?).

"How does meaning arise?" constitutes meaning as a field. Before the question, meaning was transparent. The question makes it opaque — bounded, differentiated, available for attention.

These constituting questions are called *problematizations*. They're powerful because they generate not just a field but the motivation to explore it.

## Fields Are Alive

Fields aren't static containers. They have lifecycles:

- **Latent**: Could be bounded but isn't yet
- **Emerging**: Bounding in progress, edges unclear
- **Established**: Stable, recognized, navigable
- **Contested**: Boundaries or differentiations disputed
- **Fragmenting**: Breaking into sub-fields
- **Merging**: Combining with others
- **Dissolving**: Losing boundedness, returning to flux
- **Residual**: Traces remain but field no longer active

"Doing laundry" was latent before domestic science constituted it. It's established now. It might dissolve if automation makes it invisible again.

---

## Why Fields Matter

Understanding fields reframes what it means to work in a creative domain.

You're not receiving a pre-given territory. You're constituting territory through bounding and differentiation. The fields you work in are choices — carved from flux, not found in nature.

This means:
- **Stale fields can be split** — find the fault lines and make new fields
- **Disconnected fields can be merged** — find the structural similarities
- **Narrow fields can be expanded** — move boundaries outward
- **Diffuse fields can be deepened** — add differentiation

The frame of the field shapes what sites become visible. Change the frame, change what you can find.
