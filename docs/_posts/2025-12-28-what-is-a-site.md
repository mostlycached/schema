Every creative work begins somewhere. Not with a theme or a goal, but with a *site* — a position in possibility space where something can happen.

A site isn't just an idea. It's a structured position with embedded dynamics: what movements are possible from here, what directions feel natural, what's blocked.

---

## Sites Are Positions with Embedded Gestures

Think of a site like "the pause in speech." It's not just a concept — it has built-in tendencies:

- **Available movements**: The pause can branch into many possible continuations, or resolve into speech
- **Dominant gesture**: The pause *suspends* — it holds open what comes next
- **Blocked movements**: The pause can't commit (it's inherently uncommitted)

Every site carries these dynamics. Some sites feel like they want to branch. Others feel like they're resolving. Some defer indefinitely.

---

## The Seven Gestures

Sites afford seven fundamental possibility operations:

| Gesture | What It Does | Example |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| **BRANCH** | Creates divergent paths without committing | A story could go this way *or* that way |
| **NARROW** | Reduces possibilities by adding constraints | "Now we know she's lying" |
| **COMMIT** | Forecloses alternatives irrevocably | The character dies |
| **DEFER** | Preserves options by postponing commitment | We don't learn who sent the letter |
| **SUSPEND** | Holds open a question for later resolution | What really happened that night? |
| **WIDEN** | Expands possibility space beyond current boundaries | Suddenly there's a new faction involved |
| **RESOLVE** | Collapses suspended possibilities into determinate state | The mystery is solved |

When you sit at a site, you feel which gestures are available. Some sites want to branch; others are ready to resolve. The work is often about following what the site suggests.

---

## Sites Emerge from Domains

You don't find sites wandering in the abstract. Sites emerge when a *practice* engages a *field*.

**Field**: A region of reality you can attend to. "Doing laundry." "Renaissance painting." "The concept of justice."

**Practice**: A mode of engaging that field. Writing. Analyzing. Designing.

**Domain**: When practice meets field, a domain forms — and sites become visible.

The site "the felt shift from soiled to fresh" only appears when a phenomenological practice engages the field of laundry. An analytical practice engaging the same field might reveal "the dirty/clean boundary as classification system."

Same field, different practice, different sites.

---

## Constraints Shape Which Sites Are Visible

A constraint is a generative restriction. The premise "a man wakes as an insect" doesn't narrow what you can write — it *shapes* what becomes visible.

Under that constraint:
- Sites about normal human socializing become invisible
- Sites about isolation, body horror, and family shame become visible
- The gesture SUSPEND becomes dominant (how will this resolve?)

Good constraints are fertile. They don't restrict creativity — they reveal a whole landscape of sites that weren't visible before.

---

## Sites Have Dimensional Positions

Every site exists somewhere in a multi-dimensional space:

- **Ontological**: Is this about substance, relation, absence, process, limit, tension?
- **Scale**: Sub-momentary, momentary, extended, lifetime-spanning, cosmic?
- **Temporal**: Instant, transition, oscillation, accumulation, memory, anticipation?
- **Perceptual**: Direct, peripheral, retrospective, failure-mediated?
- **Relational**: Solitary, dyadic, collective?
- **Structural**: Simple, recursive, paradoxical, incomplete?

These dimensions help you navigate. If you're at a site that feels exhausted, you might move along the perceptual dimension — finding the same site but seen through failure rather than direct perception.

---

## Finding Sites, Moving Between Sites

The work of creative exploration is moving through site-space:

1. **Enter a domain** (practice engages field)
2. **Apply constraints** (shape what's visible)
3. **Find sites** (positions with gestures)
4. **Follow gestures** (move to new sites)
5. **Derive new constraints** (from interesting sites)
6. **Repeat**

Sometimes you BRANCH, generating multiple paths without committing. Sometimes you NARROW, adding a constraint to focus. Sometimes you RESOLVE — arriving at a site that feels like a destination.

The goal isn't to find *all* sites. It's to move through a territory in a way that produces work.

---

## Why Sites Matter

Sites reframe what creative exploration is. It's not:
- Having ideas (too vague)
- Following themes (too general)
- Filling in an outline (too mechanical)

It's positioning yourself in a structured possibility space and *following the movements the position affords*.

When you're stuck, it's often because you're at a site with blocked gestures. The solution isn't to try harder — it's to move to an adjacent site where different movements are available.

When work flows, it's often because you've found a site with rich available gestures, and you're letting the gestures carry you.
