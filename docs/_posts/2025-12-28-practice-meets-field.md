There's a mystery at the heart of creative work: where do new ideas come from? Not "what causes inspiration" but more specifically: what are the conditions under which something new becomes visible?

The answer involves a meeting — of practice and field.

---

## Fields and Practices Are Different Things

**Field**: A region of reality that can be attended to.
- "Doing laundry"
- "Renaissance painting"
- "The sensation of cold"
- "The concept of justice"

Fields are *what you attend to*.

**Practice**: A mode of engagement.
- Writing
- Analyzing
- Designing
- Investigating
- Composing

Practices are *how you engage*.

These are fundamentally different. A field is territory; a practice is a way of moving through territory.

---

## Sites Emerge at the Intersection

Sites don't exist in fields waiting to be found. Sites don't exist in practices waiting to be applied.

**Sites emerge when practice engages field.**

The same field engaged through different practices yields different sites:

| Field: Doing Laundry | Practice | Sites Revealed |
|---------------------|----------|----------------|
| | Analytical | "The dirty/clean boundary as classification system" |
| | Phenomenological | "The felt weight of wet fabric" |
| | Design | "The workflow as improvable process" |
| | Meditative | "The rhythm of folding as contemplative practice" |

The same practice engaging different fields yields different sites:

| Practice: Analysis | Field | Sites Revealed |
|-------------------|-------|----------------|
| | Doing laundry | "Classification structures" |
| | Renaissance painting | "Compositional geometry" |
| | Justice | "Logical relationships between rights and duties" |

**Site = Practice × Field**

The multiplication matters. It's not practice PLUS field. It's the *product* — what emerges from their interaction.

---

## This Has Implications

### Sites Can Appear and Disappear

If you stop practicing, sites aren't there anymore. They don't persist independently.

Return with the practice and they can reappear — but might be different. The practice has changed; you've changed; the field might have changed.

### Sites Are Perspectival

Different practices engaging the same field see different sites. Neither is wrong.

The phenomenological "felt weight of wet fabric" and the analytical "classification system" are both real sites in the field of laundry. They're perspectivally real — visible from particular practice-positions.

### Sites Can Be Shared

If two people bring similar practices to the same field, they can find the same sites.

This is what traditions do: they transmit practices so sites become intersubjectively accessible. Learning to write fiction isn't just learning techniques — it's learning to see the sites that practitioners see.

---

## The Domain: Stabilized Pairing

When a practice engages a field repeatedly, the pairing stabilizes. This is a **domain**.

"Short fiction" is a domain: the practice of writing engaged with the field of narrative prose. It has:
- **Accumulated sites** (story structures, character types, techniques)
- **Developed constraints** (premises, genre conventions)
- **Characteristic gestures** (SUSPEND is common — mystery, tension)
- **Barrenness patterns** (overworked territory, fresh territory)

Your personal "laundry practice" is also a domain — whatever practice you bring (analytical, phenomenological, meditative) engaged with the field of doing laundry. Idiosyncratic, but still a domain with its own accumulation.

---

## Domains Can Nest and Overlap

**Nesting**: Domains contain sub-domains.

- Writing → Short fiction → Flash fiction
- Art → Visual art → Painting → Oil painting

The sub-domain inherits constraints and sites from the parent but adds specificity.

**Overlapping**: Domains share territory.

- "Literary fiction" and "genre fiction" overlap in narrative prose
- "Theater" and "film" overlap in dramatic performance

In overlapping zones, you can translate sites between domains — the "unreliable narrator" in fiction becomes the "subjective camera" in film.

## How Practices Manifest Constraints

The formal structure of constraints is universal: predicate, strength, fertility, dimensions targeted.

But what constraints *look like* differs by practice:

| Practice | Constraint Form | Example |
|----------|-----------------|---------|
| Writing | Premise | "A man wakes as an insect" |
| Mathematics | Axiom | "Parallel lines never meet" |
| Music | Motif | Four-note figure that recurs |
| Architecture | Parti | "Central courtyard organizing all circulation" |
| Science | Hypothesis | "Light speed is constant in all reference frames" |
| Philosophy | Problematic | "What is the nature of consciousness?" |

Same formal operation (constraint as generative restriction), different manifestation.

---

## How Practices Manifest Gestures

The seven gestures are universal, but each practice expresses them differently:

| Gesture | In Writing | In Mathematics | In Music |
|---------|-----------|----------------|----------|
| BRANCH | Open narrative possibilities | Consider cases (disjunctive proof) | Variation, alternative voicing |
| NARROW | Specify detail, close alternatives | Add hypothesis, specialize theorem | Develop theme, reduce to motif |
| COMMIT | Irreversible plot event, revelation | Assert lemma, take as proven | Cadence, arrival |
| DEFER | Withhold information, delay resolution | "Left as exercise," postpone proof | Deceptive cadence, extension |
| SUSPEND | Pose question, create tension | Conjecture, open problem | Suspension, pedal point |
| WIDEN | Expand scope, introduce new elements | Generalize, remove hypotheses | Modulation, new theme |
| RESOLVE | Answer question, complete arc | Complete proof, QED | Authentic cadence, return to tonic |

Learning a practice means learning how to perform these gestures in its terms.

## Switching Practice, Switching Field

Two operations for creative movement:

### Switch Practice

Keep the field, change how you engage it.

You've been analyzing "the committee meeting" (field). You switch to phenomenological practice. Now you're attending to "the felt experience of the meeting" — different sites become visible. The power dynamics you analyzed are still there, but now you're noticing the texture of the uncomfortable chair, the quality of light, the rhythm of turn-taking.

### Switch Field

Keep the practice, change what you engage.

You've been writing (practice) about "the tea ceremony" (field). You switch to "the emergency room" (field). Same practice, new territory. Some of what you learned transfers — how to SUSPEND tension, how to BRANCH into perspectives — but new sites appear that require new responses.

## Translation and Bridging

### Translating Sites

A site found in one domain can sometimes be translated to another.

"The moment of becoming clean" (found in analytical practice × laundry) might translate to "the felt shift from soiled to fresh" (phenomenological practice × laundry).

Translation isn't automatic. It requires:
1. Identifying what's practice-specific vs. field-specific in the site
2. Preserving the field-specific content
3. Re-engaging with the target practice
4. Seeing if the site reappears in new form

### Bridging Sites

Some sites exist at the intersection of multiple domains.

"The premise as generative constraint" bridges (writing × narrative) and (mathematics × formal systems). It exists in both domains, revealing something common to them.

Bridging sites are precious. They're how knowledge transfers between domains. Finding them is one of the most generative moves you can make.

---

## Why This Matters

The practice-meets-field model changes how you approach creative work:

**If a domain feels exhausted**: Maybe you've extracted the sites available to your current practice. Try switching practice while keeping the field, or switching field while keeping the practice.

**If you can't find sites**: Maybe you're in a field but practicing nothing. Make explicit what practice you're bringing. Then engage.

**If sites keep dissolving**: Maybe the practice isn't stabilized enough. Develop the practice — learn more techniques, internalize more gestures.

**If you want to transfer insight**: Look for bridging sites. What in this domain is structurally similar to something in another domain? That similarity is a site that can carry knowledge.
