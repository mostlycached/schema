Myth and metaphor have shaped human thought for millennia. But they're instances of something more general — three abstract operations that underlie all meaning-making.

Understanding these operations gives you conscious access to what was previously automatic.

---

## What Myth and Metaphor Were Doing

**Myth** provided:
- Directionality (toward/away)
- Significance (matters/doesn't matter)
- Narrative organization (before/after, because/therefore)

**Metaphor** provided:
- Cross-domain mapping (this is like that)
- Structural transfer (what holds here holds there)
- Intelligibility (understand X through Y)

But these are instances of deeper operations.

---

## Operation 1: Mapping

The most basic operation: *relating two things such that structure transfers*.

Metaphor is one kind of mapping. But there are others:
- **Analogy**: Structural similarity
- **Homology**: Shared origin
- **Isomorphism**: Exact structural correspondence
- **Embedding**: One inside another
- **Projection**: Reduction that preserves some structure
- **Translation**: Re-encoding in different medium

What's common: **M: A → B** — structure in A illuminates or generates structure in B.

### Questions About Any Mapping

When you map, you're deciding:
- **What transfers?** Elements? Relations? Operations? Constraints?
- **How does it transfer?** Exactly (isomorphism)? With transformation? Partially? With loss?
- **Which direction?** A → B only? Or bidirectional?
- **Where does it break?** Every mapping fails somewhere. Where?

The failure points are often the most interesting sites. Where the mapping breaks reveals something about both source and target.

---

## Operation 2: Valuation

The operation of *assigning significance* — making some things matter more than others.

Myth did this through narrative significance. But there are other ways:
- **Preference**: I want this more than that
- **Importance**: This matters more than that
- **Salience**: This stands out more than that
- **Stakes**: More is at risk here than there
- **Care**: I care about this more than that

What's common: **V: X → ℝ** — things get ranked. Some things are higher, some lower.

### Questions About Any Valuation

When you valuate, you're deciding:
- **What's the ground?** Why this valuation? (Because it serves something? Because of relation? Just is?)
- **What type?** Cardinal (how much more)? Ordinal (just more)? Boolean (matters/doesn't)?
- **How stable?** Fixed forever? Shifting? Volatile?
- **Is it revisable?** Can the valuation itself change?

Valuation creates landscape. High-value regions attract attention. Low-value regions become invisible. Value gradients create boundaries.

---

## Operation 3: Orientation

The operation of *establishing directionality* — making some directions more salient than others.

Telos (purpose, aim, toward-which) is one kind of orientation. But there are others:
- **Origin**: Where from
- **Destination**: Where to
- **Trajectory**: The path
- **Gradient**: Direction of increase/decrease
- **Attractor**: What pulls
- **Repulsor**: What pushes away
- **Current**: What carries

What's common: **O: Space → VectorField** — points get associated with directions.

### Questions About Any Orientation

When you orient:
- **What type?** Point attractor (destination)? Gradient (direction of increase)? Basin (where things flow)?
- **What's the source?** Purpose? Origin? Value gradient? External force?
- **How strong?** Weak pull? Strong gravity?
- **How stable?** Fixed? Shifting? Pulsing?

Orientation creates flow. Some directions feel natural. Others feel upstream.

---

## The Three Operations Can Combine

The operations are independent but composable:

**Mapping without valuation**: Structure transfers but nothing matters more. Pure formal analogy.

**Valuation without orientation**: Things matter but there's no direction. A landscape with no preferred path.

**Orientation without valuation**: There's direction but no significance. Moving but not caring.

**Mapping + Valuation**: "This is like that, *and it matters in the same way*." The transferred structure carries its significance.

**Mapping + Orientation**: "This is like that, *and the direction is the same*." The transferred structure carries its flow.

**Valuation + Orientation**: "This matters, *and there's a direction*." Value gradient creates the pull.

**All three together**: Structure transfers, things matter, there's direction. This is what myth-and-metaphor were doing — all three operations at once.

## How the Operations Generate

Each operation is generative — it produces sites.

### Mapping Generates

When you map A → B:
- Elements in A suggest elements in B
- Relations in A suggest relations in B
- **Gaps in the mapping suggest sites** (where does it break?)
- **The mapping itself is a site**

Example: You map "ecosystem" onto "office dynamics." Predator-prey relations map onto certain power dynamics. Resource flow maps onto information flow. But where does the mapping fail? Maybe there's nothing in "ecosystem" that corresponds to "the meeting." That gap is a site — something about offices that ecosystems don't have.

### Valuation Generates

When you assign values:
- High-value regions attract attention, become sites
- Low-value regions become invisible (or become sites *when revalued*)
- **Value boundaries create sites** (where value transitions sharply)
- **Conflicts between valuations create sites**

Example: You value "honesty" and "kindness" differently. Sometimes they conflict — a situation demands one or the other. That conflict is a site.

### Orientation Generates

When you establish direction:
- Attractors become sites (destinations)
- Origins become sites (sources)
- **Trajectories suggest sites along the path**
- **Cross-currents and eddies become sites**

Example: The story is oriented toward "resolution of the mystery." But there's a counter-current — a subplot pulling toward "the detective's own breakdown." The eddy where these currents meet is a site.

## Revisiting Familiar Concepts

The three operations illuminate what was already in the framework:

### Constraints as All Three

A constraint is: **Mapping** (from general to specific) + **Valuation** (what satisfies matters) + **Orientation** (toward satisfaction).

The premise "a man wakes as an insect":
- **Mapping**: From all narrative possibilities to Gregor-as-insect possibilities
- **Valuation**: Developments consistent with premise matter more
- **Orientation**: Toward the implications of insect-being

### Gestures as All Three

Each gesture involves all three operations:

**BRANCH**:
- Mapping: ONE → MANY (unfolds)
- Valuation: Spreads value across branches
- Orientation: Creates multiple directions from single point

**COMMIT**:
- Mapping: MANY → ONE (collapses)
- Valuation: All value to committed option
- Orientation: Single direction, irreversible

**SUSPEND**:
- Mapping: Creates unmapped region (question)
- Valuation: Values the question itself
- Orientation: Toward resolution

### Flux as Absence of All Three

Flux is the state where:
- **No mapping**: Nothing is related to anything else
- **No valuation**: Nothing matters more than anything else
- **No orientation**: No direction, no toward/away

To enter flux is to release mapping, valuation, and orientation. To emerge from flux is to precipitate all three.

## Practical Use

Knowing the three operations gives you diagnostic power:

**If work feels disconnected**: Maybe there's no mapping. Consider: what structural analogies could illuminate this?

**If work feels flat**: Maybe there's no valuation. Consider: what matters more here? Where are the stakes?

**If work feels directionless**: Maybe there's no orientation. Consider: what's pulling? What's the toward?

**If work feels forced**: Maybe valuation or orientation is arbitrary. Consider: does the significance come from the material, or was it imposed?

**If work feels ungrounded**: Maybe mapping is partial or breaking. Consider: where does the analogy fail? What does the failure reveal?

## The Deep Structure

The three operations are the infrastructure of meaning-making:

| Operation | What it does | Input | Output |
|-----------|--------------|-------|--------|
| **Mapping** | Relates structures | Two structures | Transfer relation |
| **Valuation** | Assigns significance | Structure | Significance gradient |
| **Orientation** | Establishes direction | Space | Vector field |

Myth and metaphor bundled all three. Unbundling them gives you conscious access to each. You can see where your meaning-making relies on one operation more than others, and adjust.
