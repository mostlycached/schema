## The Abstract Machine

We have defined the concrete elements of the system: Fields, Practices, Sites, Constraints.
Now we ask: **What are the fundamental operations that make these work?**

The system runs on two layers:
1.  **The Infrastructure (MVA)**: The static variables that define a state.
2.  **The Engine (The Parasite)**: The dynamic operator that transforms the state.

---

## Part I: The Infrastructure (MVA)

Every site, field, and constraint is composed of three primitive operations.

### 1. Mapping (Relationships)
**What it does**: Relates two structures (A → B).
**Function**: Establishes *connection*.
*   *Types*: Analogy, Homology, Embedding, Translation.
*   *Failure*: When a mapping breaks, it reveals a "Gap" (a fertile site).

### 2. Valuation (Significance)
**What it does**: Assigns rank (X > Y).
**Function**: Establishes *priority*.
*   *Types*: Narrative importance, Emotional weight, Stakes, Attention.
*   *Topography*: Valuation creates "Peaks" (high attention) and "Valleys" (invisible zones).

### 3. Orientation (Direction)
**What it does**: Establishes a vector (Point → Direction).
**Function**: Establishes *flow*.
*   *Types*: Telos (Goal), Gravity (Attractor), Gradient (Path of least resistance).

### The MVA State
Any momentary state of a system can be described as a specific configuration of **M**appings, **V**aluations, and **O**rientations.
*   *Flux* is the absence of MVA.
*   *Order* is a stable MVA configuration.

## Part II: The Engine (The Parasite)

If MVA defines the state, what forces the state to change?
**The Parasite.**

Derived from Michel Serres, the Parasite is not just a biological leech. It is the **Operator of Interception**. It is the "Third" that disrupts the relations between "Two."

### 1. Interception (Breaking the Mapping)
The Parasite positions itself on the line between A and B.
*   *Standard MVA*: Mapping A → B.
*   *Parasitic Action*: A → Parasite → B.
*   *Result*: The mapping is interrupted. The relation must complexify to include the Third.

### 2. The Thermal Exciter (Shifting the Valuation)
The Parasite introduces **Noise**.
*   *Standard MVA*: Valuation prefers Signal.
*   *Parasitic Action*: Noise increases.
*   *Result*: The system must expend energy to deal with the noise. It must **re-value** the environment to include the disturbance.

### 3. The Cascade (Redirecting the Orientation)
Parasitism is recursive.
*   *Generative Loop*: The Host tries to expel the Parasite. This effort creates a new niche for a *new* Parasite.
*   *Result*: The Orientation shifts from "Maintenance" to "Defense" to "Transformation."

---

## Synthesis: How It Works

We use **The Parasite** to operate on **MVA**.

| MVA Layer | Parasitic Operation | Resulting Mutation |
|-----------|---------------------|--------------------|
| **Mapping** | **Interception** | The dyad becomes a triad. A new "Included Third" creates new links. |
| **Valuation** | **Noise Injection** | What was "waste" (noise) becomes "value" (signal). The hierarchy flips. |
| **Orientation** | **Deviation** | The flow is diverted. The system builds a detour (complexity) around the block. |

### Example: The Unwanted Guest

Consider a Dinner Party. Ideally, it is a closed loop of hospitality.

1.  **Original MVA (The Ideal Party)**:
    *   *Mapping*: Host ↔ Guest (Reciprocity).
    *   *Valuation*: Harmony > Interruption.
    *   *Orientation*: Toward strict etiquette.

2.  **Parasitic Intervention**:
    *   *Parasite*: A guest gets drunk and spills red wine on the white tablecloth.
    *   *Interception*: The Stain is the Third. All eyes move from the Host to the Stain. The conversation stops.

3.  **New MVA (The Reorganization)**:
    *   *Mapping*: Host ↔ Stain ↔ Guest. The Dyad must now collaborate to clean the mess.
    *   *Valuation*: Cleaning > Eating. The trivial (the cloth) becomes the highest value.
    *   *Orientation*: Crisis Management. The party has mutated. It is no longer about food; it is about *The Incident*.

**Result**: The "Noise" (The Stain) forced the system to generate a new complexity (the cleaning ritual) to survive.

## Applied Parasitism: Catalog Case Studies

### Case Study A: The Visceral Interceptor
**Site**: [076 - The Thing You Found in the Food](./catalog/076-the-thing-you-found-in-the-food.md)

This site maps the exact moment the Parasite enters the most intimate mapping (Eating).
*   **Original MVA**: `Food → Mouth` (Trust/Nourishment).
*   **Interception**: The "Thing" (Hair/Bug/Glass) appears.
*   **Mutation**: The mapping breaks. The value of the food drops to zero (or negative). The orientation shifts from "Consumption" to "Inspection/Rejection."
*   **Generative Result**: "Nausea" is the system's attempt to expel the Parasite.

### Case Study B: The Included Third
**Site**: [005 - The Third Thing in the Room](./catalog/005-third-thing-in-room.md)

This site explicitly models the Parasite as an atmospheric agent.
*   **Original MVA**: `Person A ↔ Person B`.
*   **The Third**: An unspoken history or tension that sits between them.
*   **Mutation**: The Third Thing generates gestures (glances, silences) that A and B must perform. They are no longer interacting with each other; they are interacting with *It*.
*   **Generative Result**: The relationship is triangulated. The Third Thing dictates the rules.

*   **MVA** (Mapping, Valuation, Orientation) is the language of **Structure**.
*   **The Parasite** (Interception, Noise, Canalization) is the language of **Change**.
*   **Constraint Closure** (Chapter 7) builds the trap; **The Parasite** (Chapter 8) breaks it (or makes it evolve).
*   Novelty arises when the System integrates the Parasite.

## Exercises

### Warm-up

**8.1** Analyze your current workspace (physical or digital).
- **Mapping**: What connects to what? (Computer → Monitor, Chair → Desk)
- **Valuation**: What matters most? (The screen? The coffee?)
- **Orientation**: Where does the setup point you? (Toward work? Toward distraction?)

**8.2** Identify a "Parasite" in your daily routine.
- Not a biological one, but an interrupter. A notification, a worry, a recurring pain.
- How does it intercept your mappings?
- Does it create new noise that you have to re-value?

---

### Standard

**8.3** **Parasitize a Valuation**.
- Take a site where `Speed > Quality` (e.g., a rush hour commute, or fast food).
- Introduce a Parasite that feeds on Speed (e.g., "The Traffic Jam" or "The Missing Ingredient").
- How does the Valuation shift? Does "Patience" suddenly become high value? Does "Escape" become high value?

**8.4** **The Cascade Map**.
- Trace a chain of at least three interceptions in a recent event.
- `A ← Parasite 1` (The Plan was intercepted by Rain)
- `Parasite 1 ← Parasite 2` (The Rain was intercepted by a Leaking Roof)
- `Parasite 2 ← Parasite 3` (The Leak was intercepted by searching for a Bucket)
- How far did you get from the original Orientation?

**8.5** Design a "Thermal Exciter" for a boring conversation.
- The system is stable (low energy, low information).
- Introduce a noise element (a controversial statement, a sudden silence, a strange object).
- Watch the system heat up (expend energy) to deal with the noise. Does it re-value the conversation?

---

### Challenge

**8.6** **The Autonomy Paradox**.
- Design a site that is heavily parasitized but *functions better* because of it.
- Example: The immune system (needs pathogens to learn).
- Example: The jazz improvisation (needs accident to find new form).
- Sketch the MVA of such a system. How does it metabolize noise into signal?

**8.7** **The Perfect Trap (Constraint Closure)**.
- Using the "Infinite Debt" model, design a trap for a specific user type (e.g., The Perfectionist).
- **Process**: What do they try to do? (Check for errors).
- **Constraint A**: The Standard (Must be perfect).
- **Constraint B**: The Multiplier (Checking generates more doubts).
- How does their attempt to escape (checking) regenerate the wall (doubt)?
