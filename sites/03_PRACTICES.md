# Chapter 3: Practices — Modes of Engagement

> **Prerequisites**: [Chapter 2: Fields](./02_FIELDS.md)
> 
> **Key Concepts**: Practice, Field Levels, Constraint Forms, Gesture Manifestations, Flux Capacity
>
> **Learning Objectives**: By the end, you will understand practices as modes of engaging with fields, and how the same field yields different sites under different practices.

---

## What a Practice Is

A practice is a **mode of engaging with fields**.

Examples of practices:
- Writing
- Analyzing
- Designing
- Investigating
- Composing
- Phenomenological attention
- Engineering
- Meditation

Practices are not topics or subjects. They're ways of approaching — methods of engagement that shape what becomes visible.

---

## The Key Insight

Fields don't contain sites waiting to be found.

**Sites emerge when a practice engages a field.**

The same field, engaged through different practices, yields different sites. This is crucial. Let's see it in action.

---

## Same Field, Different Practices

Consider the field "doing laundry":

| Practice | What Becomes Visible |
|----------|---------------------|
| Analytical | Classification structures (dirty/clean, whites/colors) |
| Phenomenological | Felt textures (weight of wet fabric, smell of detergent) |
| Design | Workflow as improvable process (optimize steps) |
| Meditative | Rhythm as contemplative practice (folding as attention) |
| Sociological | Labor distribution, gender norms, domestic economy |
| Engineering | Mechanical processes, resource optimization |

Each practice reveals sites the others don't see. "The felt weight of wet fabric" (phenomenological) and "the dirty/clean classification system" (analytical) are both real sites in the field of laundry — but visible from different practice-positions.

---

## Which Levels Practices Engage

Recall that fields have levels (from Chapter 2):

| Level | What It Contains |
|-------|------------------|
| Material | Physical stuff |
| Phenomenal | Experienced stuff |
| Conceptual | Abstract stuff |
| Social | Intersubjective stuff |

Different practices engage different levels:

| Practice | Primary Levels |
|----------|---------------|
| Engineering | Material, Conceptual |
| Phenomenology | Phenomenal |
| Sociology | Social, Conceptual |
| Design | All levels |
| Writing (fiction) | Phenomenal, Conceptual, Social |
| Mathematics | Conceptual |
| Contemplative | Phenomenal (pre-conceptual) |

A practice can only reveal sites at the levels it engages.

## Constraint Forms

In Chapter 7, you'll learn about constraints in detail. For now, the key point: **constraints take different forms in different practices**.

The formal structure is the same (a generative restriction that shapes which sites are visible). But what they *look like* differs:

| Practice | Constraint Form | Example |
|----------|-----------------|---------|
| Writing | Premise | "A man wakes as an insect" |
| Mathematics | Axiom | "Parallel lines never meet" |
| Music | Motif | Four-note recurring figure |
| Architecture | Parti | "Central courtyard organizing circulation" |
| Science | Hypothesis | "Light speed is constant" |
| Philosophy | Problematic | "What is consciousness?" |
| Design | Brief | "Must support 3 user types" |

Same operation (constraint as generative restriction), different manifestation. Learning a practice means learning how constraints appear in its terms.

## Gesture Manifestations

In Chapter 6, you'll learn the seven gestures in detail. For now: **gestures also manifest differently by practice**.

### Writing

| Gesture | Manifestation |
|---------|---------------|
| BRANCH | Open narrative possibilities without committing |
| NARROW | Specify detail, close off alternatives |
| COMMIT | Irreversible plot event, revelation |
| DEFER | Withhold information, delay resolution |
| SUSPEND | Pose question, create tension |
| WIDEN | Expand scope, introduce new elements |
| RESOLVE | Answer question, complete arc |

### Mathematics

| Gesture | Manifestation |
|---------|---------------|
| BRANCH | Consider cases, disjunctive proof |
| NARROW | Add hypothesis, specialize theorem |
| COMMIT | Assert lemma, take as proven |
| DEFER | "Left as exercise," postpone proof |
| SUSPEND | Conjecture, open problem |
| WIDEN | Generalize, remove hypotheses |
| RESOLVE | Complete proof, QED |

### Music

| Gesture | Manifestation |
|---------|---------------|
| BRANCH | Variation, alternative voicing |
| NARROW | Develop theme, reduce to motif |
| COMMIT | Cadence, arrival |
| DEFER | Deceptive cadence, extension |
| SUSPEND | Suspension, pedal point |
| WIDEN | Modulation, new theme |
| RESOLVE | Authentic cadence, return to tonic |

Learning a practice means learning how to perform gestures in its vocabulary.

## Flux Capacity

Different practices have different relationships to flux (from Chapter 1):

| Practice | Can Dissolve | Can Open | Can Hover | Typical Risks |
|----------|--------------|----------|-----------|---------------|
| Phenomenology | Field, Domain, Site | All levels | Yes | Self-dissolution |
| Engineering | Limited (specs) | Within domain | Limited | Rigidification |
| Creative writing | Site, Constraint | Domain, Site | Yes | Chaos |
| Meditation (some) | All including self | All levels | Yes | Formlessness |
| Deconstruction | Field, Domain | All levels | Essential | Self-dissolution |
| Improv | Site, Constraint | All levels | Yes | Chaos |

Some practices give deliberate access to flux. Others never approach it. This shapes what kind of creative movement is possible within the practice.

---

## Practice Lineages

Practices have histories. They develop, branch, merge:

- **Writing** contains fiction, non-fiction, poetry, drama...
- **Fiction writing** contains literary fiction, genre fiction, experimental...
- **Genre writing** contains mystery, romance, science fiction...

Child practices inherit from parent practices but add specificity. You can practice at any level of specificity — "writing" (broad) or "hardboiled detective fiction" (narrow).

---

## Summary

- **Practices** are modes of engaging with fields
- **Sites emerge** when practice meets field — not before
- **Same field, different practices** → different sites
- Practices engage different **field levels** (material, phenomenal, conceptual, social)
- **Constraints take practice-specific forms** (premise, axiom, motif, etc.)
- **Gestures manifest differently** by practice
- Practices have different **flux capacities**
- Practices form **lineages** (parent/child relationships)

## Exercises

### Warm-up

**3.1 The Dinner Table**

**Position**: You are eating dinner with your family. Apply three different practices:

| Practice | Level | Reveals |
|----------|-------|---------|
| **Phenomenological** | Phenomenal | The scrape of the fork, the saltiness, the tension in a jaw |
| **Sociological** | Social | Gender roles (who serves?), power dynamics (who interrupts?) |
| **Design** | Material/Functional | Poor table layout, inefficient passing routes |

**Question**: Which practice would be *strangest* to actually adopt at the table? (Hint: Design. Treating family dinner as logistics ignores the social contract.)

**3.2 The Level Shift**

**Position**: You're in a conversation. You realize you've shifted from Social level (what should be said) to Phenomenal level (what you're actually feeling).

| Before | After | Trigger |
|--------|-------|---------|
| Social (Words) | Phenomenal (Sensation) | Fatigue, emotion, drugs, or intense staring |

**Question**: What does this shift *feel* like? (Hint: vertigo. The words stop meaning things and become sounds. The face becomes meat.)

---

### Standard

**3.3 The Implicit Practice**

**Position**: Pick something you do regularly that isn't named as a "practice" (scrolling, commuting, showering).

| Activity | Implicit Gestures |
|----------|-------------------|
| **Commuting** | NARROW (ignore homeless person), COMMIT (choose lane), DEFER (podcast to not be "here") |

**Question**: Once you name the implicit gestures, what *is* the practice? (Example: Commuting = "The Practice of Erasure" — navigating space while pretending not to exist.)

**3.4 Flux Capacity**

**Position**: What practice are you most fluent in? What's its flux capacity?

| Practice Type | Flux Capacity | Examples |
|---------------|---------------|----------|
| **Analytical** | Low (solid concepts) | Criticism, Debugging |
| **Improvisational** | High (can dissolve) | Jazz, Improv Comedy |
| **Meditative** | Very High | Vipassana, Floating |

**Question**: What's *disorienting* about entering a practice with different flux capacity than your native one? (Hint: the lack of handholds. In high-flux, you can't "lean" on rules.)

**3.5 The Practice Switch**

**Position**: You're stuck in a project. The practice you're using isn't generating sites.

| Original Practice | Stuck Symptom | Switch To | New Sites |
|-------------------|---------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Analyzing** | No new insights | Composing (aesthetic) | Rhythm, form |
| **Designing** | No new forms | Walking (somatic) | Sensation, pace |

**Barrier**: The belief that the new practice is "not working" (i.e., not analyzing). You must value the new output (a rhythm) even if it looks like waste to the old practice.

---

### Challenge

**3.6 Therapy Design**

**Position**: Standard talk therapy engages Conceptual/Social levels, low flux.

| Property | Standard Therapy | Alt-Therapy (Design) |
|----------|------------------|----------------------|
| Levels | Conceptual, Social | Phenomenal, Material |
| Flux | Low (seeks stability) | High (dissolves "self") |
| Reveals | The "story" | Where trauma lives in the fascia |

**Question**: Design an alt-therapy. What levels does it engage? What does it reveal that the standard form misses?

**3.7 The Invisible Practice**

**Position**: You've internalized a practice so deeply you can't see it. It's become "just how you see things."

| Example | The Practice | How to Reveal |
|---------|--------------|---------------|
| Always optimizing | "Efficiency" | Try to do something purely for waste. Feel the anxiety. |
| Always reading status | "Hierarchy" | Enter a room and ignore who has power. What happens? |

**Question**: Name one of your own invisible practices. What would you see differently if you stepped outside it?
