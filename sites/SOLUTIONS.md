## Chapter 1: Flux

### 1.1

You're **emerging** — improvising a new structure in real-time because the original dissolved. This isn't hovering (you're not holding a boundary); it's active production. The forgotten ending created a flux pocket, and you're precipitating new material to fill it.

The giveaway is: you might end up somewhere different than the actual movie. That's emergence, not retrieval.

---

### 1.2

**The party**: The room has no structure *for you*. Other people have their structures (they know where to stand, who to talk to). You're in flux relative to the social space. What dissolved? Your usual position — where you'd naturally be, who you'd naturally talk to. 

**Your kitchen**: Everything is pre-structured. You know where things are, what the room is *for*, where your body goes automatically. The kitchen is solidified structure — probably rigidified.

The comparison reveals: structure isn't in the room, it's in *your relationship to the room*. Same room, different person = different flux state.

---

### 1.3

The itch paradox: attending to the itch *doesn't dissolve it*. In fact, attention often intensifies structure. The itch gets more defined, more located, more demanding.

What dissolves the itch? *Distraction* — attending elsewhere. Or transformation — the itch becomes "sensation" without the pull-toward-scratching. That's a re-emergence: same raw material, different structure.

The lesson: **attention isn't dissolution**. You can't stare a structure into flux. You can only shift what you're attending to, or attend in a way that doesn't reinforce.

---

### 1.4

**Phase transitions in language learning**:
1. Noise → words: You hear a boundary. Repetition crystallizes the sound-chunk into a recognizable unit.
2. Words → meaning: The word becomes transparent. You hear *through* it to what it refers to.

**Dissolving your native language back to noise** would require:
- Extreme repetition (say a word 100 times — it becomes strange)
- Attention to physical production (how your mouth moves)
- Context removal (words in isolation, no meaning-supporting context)

Native speakers occasionally experience this briefly — semantic satiation. But it's unstable. The structure snaps back fast because it's deeply crystallized.

---

### 1.5

**What maintains the pre-sleep HOVER**:
- Low stakes (nothing you need to do)
- Warmth, comfort (body not demanding attention)
- Low light (reduces visual structure)
- The *intention* to sleep (you're not trying to structure anything)

**Tips into structure** (awake):
- A worry with action-demand ("Did I lock the door?")
- Physical discomfort
- External stimulus (sound, light)

**Tips into flux** (sleep):
- Thoughts become less coherent
- Body sensations fade
- The "I" that's attending dissolves

The HOVER is maintained by *not needing to do anything*. The moment need appears, you snap to structure.

---

### 1.6

The accidental OPEN happens when automaticity fails — a glitch. To do it *on purpose* without breaking the action:

**Strategy**: Attend to a dimension that's normally transparent.

Example approaches:
- **Feel your hands** while making coffee. Don't think about the steps — feel the grip, temperature, weight.
- **Slow one thing** (not everything). Pour at half speed. The slowness defamiliarizes.
- **Describe out loud** what you're doing, as if to an alien. The verbal encoding forces you to structure what's normally unstructured.

The key: you're adding a layer of attention that doesn't interfere with the action's execution but does interfere with its automaticity.

---

### 1.7

**Suppressing vs. dissolving**:
- *Suppressing*: The irritation-structure is still there; you're blocking expression. Costs energy. Doesn't last.
- *Dissolving*: The structure itself becomes flux. No irritation to suppress.

**Approach using flux operations**:

1. **OPEN** when the trigger occurs: Don't suppress the irritation. Don't express it. Just attend to it as *sensation*, not as "they're doing the thing again." What does irritation feel like in your body? Where? 

2. **HOVER** at the edge: Stay with the sensation but don't follow the story ("They always do this, they don't respect me..."). The story is the structure. The sensation is closer to flux.

3. **Wait for EMERGE**: If you hold the HOVER, something else might precipitate. Maybe curiosity ("Why does this bother me?"). Maybe nothing. But you're not forcing new structure.

**Can you dissolve while the trigger keeps recurring?**

Hard. Each recurrence re-crystallizes. But with practice, the structure weakens. The trigger happens; the irritation doesn't fully form. You're catching it mid-crystallization.

## Chapter 2: Fields

### 2.1
**Where anger ends**:
- **Temporal**: It ends when the physiological arousal drops. (Useful for management).
- **Phenomenal**: It ends when the world stops looking hostile. (Useful for understanding).
- **Conceptual**: It ends when "injustice" is redressed. (Useful for political action).

The most useful bounding depends on your goal. If you want to sleep, temporal bounding (wait it out) is best. If you want to fix the relationship, conceptual bounding (address the cause) is best.

### 2.2
**Differentiating Friendship**:
1.  **By maintenance cost**: High-maintenance vs. Low-maintenance friends. Reveals sites of depletion.
2.  **By information asymmetry**: Friends who know your secrets vs. Friends whose secrets you know. Reveals power dynamics.
3.  **By temporal origin**: "Work friends" vs. "Childhood friends." Reveals sites of regression/growth.

### 2.3
**Splitting a Stable Field (e.g., Parenting)**:
- **Fault Line 1**: Custodial (keeping them alive) vs. Developmental (helping them grow). These often conflict.
- **Fault Line 2**: Public (performance of good parent) vs. Private (actual relationship).
- **Split**: "Public Parenting" operates by shame/pride logic. "Private Parenting" operates by love/exhaustion logic.

### 2.4
**The Party Field Shift**:
- **Shift**: You realize you're Working while they're Playing.
- **Result**: Immediate alienation. Your field (Networking) has different constraints (don't get drunk, impress people) than theirs (relax, be authentic).
- **Resolution**: You either *Contract* (isolate yourself), *Merge* (try to play-work), or *switch fields* (stop working).

### 2.5
**Bridging Grief**:
1.  **Obvious**: Grief → Depression (Shared structure: sadness, withdrawal).
2.  **Less Obvious**: Grief → Architecture (Ruins: structures that persist after function is gone).
3.  **Strange**: Grief → Digestion (Metabolizing a heavy object: requires energy, time, and breaks down the object into the self).

### 2.6
**Phenomenal-Only Field**:
- **Field**: " The hum of the refrigerator."
- **Bounding**: Auditory only. No concept ("appliance"), no time ("evening"), no social ("kitchen"). Just the sound.
- **Stability**: Very low. Without conceptual anchors, the mind drifts. It dissolves back into "background noise" (flux) or jumps to "I need a snack" (conceptual/biological field).

### 2.7
**Merging Work/Home Self**:
- **Merge**: "The Project Manager of the Soul."
- **New Structure**: Applying efficiency metrics to childcare; applying unconditional regard to spreadsheets.
- **Emerged Sites**: "The inefficient love" (revealed as waste). "The profitable rest" (revealed as investment). The merge usually reveals the *inhumanity* of the work field or the *inefficiency* of the home field.

## Chapter 3: Practices

### 3.1
**Dinner Table Practices**:
- **Phenomenological**: You notice the scrape of the fork, the saltiness, the tension in a jaw. (Reveals: somatic reality).
- **Sociological**: You notice gender roles (who serves?), power dynamics (who interrupts?). (Reveals: hierarchy).
- **Design**: You notice poor table layout, inefficient passing routes. (Reveals: friction).
- **Strangest**: Design. Treating family dinner as a logistics problem to be optimized is alienating because it ignores the Social level (connection).

### 3.2
**Level Shifting (Social → Phenomenal)**:
- **Feel**: Vertigo. The words stop meaning things and become sounds. The face becomes meat.
- **Trigger**: Fatigue, drugs, extreme repetition, or intense emotion that breaks the social container.
- **Control**: Hard to induce at will, but "staring" (prolonged attention) often triggers it.

### 3.3
**Implicit Practice (e.g., Commuting)**:
- **Gestures**:
    - **NARROW**: Ignoring the homeless person (closing perception).
    - **COMMIT**: Choosing a lane (irreversible for 500m).
    - **DEFER**: Listening to a podcast to not be "here."
- **Explicit**: Once named, "Commuting" becomes "The Practice of Erasure" — a skilled navigating of space while pretending not to exist.

### 3.4
**Flux Capacity**:
- **Fluent**: Likely "Analytical" or "Social" (low flux capacity - deals in solid concepts).
- **Different**: Improvisation or Meditation (high flux capacity).
- **Disorientation**: The lack of handholds. In high-flux practices, you can't "lean" on rules. You have to float.

### 3.5
**Practice Switch (Stuck Project)**:
- **Situation**: Stuck Analyzing (conceptual level).
- **Switch**: Composing (aesthetic level) or Walking (somatic level).
- **Barrier**: The belief that the new practice is "not working" (i.e., not analyzing). You have to value the new output (a sensation, a rhythm) which looks like waste to the old practice.

### 3.6
**Therapy Design**:
- **Standard Therapy**: Conceptual/Social levels. Low flux (seeks stability).
- **Alt-Therapy**: "Somatic Deconstruction."
    - **Levels**: Phenomenal/Material (body sensation only).
    - **Flux**: High (dissolve "self" into sensation).
    - **Reveals**: Where trauma lives in the fascia, ignoring the "story" completely.

### 3.7
**Invisible Practice**:
- **Example**: "Optimization." You automatically look for the fastest/cheapest way.
- **Reveal**: When you try to do something purely for joy or waste, and feel anxiety.
- **Stepping Out**: Adopting "Inefficiency" as a practice. Walking the long way. Wasting ingredients. You see that "Speed" was a lens, not reality.

## Chapter 6: Gestures

### 6.1
**The Gaffe**:
- **Available**: *RESOLVE* (Apologize), *WIDEN* (Make it a joke), *DEFER* (Ignore it).
- **Dominant**: *DEFER* (Social pressure to pretend it didn't happen).
- **Blocked**: *NARROW* (You can't "un-say" it).

### 6.2
**The Deadline**:
- **Forces**: *COMMIT*. (You must decide).
- **Blocks**: *DEFER*, *SUSPEND*, *WIDEN* (No more time to explore).
- **Effect**: It forces a collapse of possibility into actuality.

### 6.3
**Chronic DEFER**:
- **Blocking COMMIT**: Usually *Fear of Loss*. Committing kills the other options.
- **Intervention**: *Artificial NARROW*. Reduce options to 2. Then the cost of COMMIT is lower. Or visualize the *decay* of the deferred options (they are dying anyway).

### 6.4
**Blocked RESOLVE**:
- **Site**: "Chronic Illness" or "Grief."
- **Acceptance**: Realizing it's a *Condition*, not a *Problem*. Problems have solutions (RESOLVE). Conditions have states.
- **Available**: *WIDEN* (Integrate the condition into a larger life). *DEEPEN* (Understand it better).

### 6.5
**Sequence**:
1.  *Stale Relationship* → **WIDEN** (Open relationship/Separate varations) → *The Open Marriage*.
2.  *The Open Marriage* → **SUSPEND** (Jealousy arises, question "Do you love me?" is active) → *The Anxious Dyad*.
3.  *The Anxious Dyad* → **COMMIT** (Affair partner becomes primary) → *The New Relationship*.
4.  *The New Relationship* → **NARROW** (Old patterns re-emerge) → *Stale Relationship*.
- **Result**: Loop. Psychological attractor.

### 6.6
**DEFER's Pair**:
- **Pair**: *ACCELERATE* (or *FORCE*).
- **DEFER**: Holds time back.
- **ACCELERATE**: Rushes time forward.
- **Concept**: Pushing for a prematurity vs. waiting for a rot.

### 6.7
**Refusing Dominant Gesture**:
- **Site**: "The Argument" (Dominant: *COMMIT/ATTACK*).
- **Refusal**: Execute *SUSPEND* (Silence).
- **Result**: *Destabilization*. The other person expects the attack (Ping Pong). Silence breaks the rhythm. They may over-commit to fill the void. The site morphs from "Conflict" to "Interrogation."

### 11.3

- **Symptom**: "I just stopped caring."

- **Cause**: *Premature Resolution* or *External Resolution Needed*.
