# Chapter 4: Domains — Where Sites Emerge

> **Prerequisites**: [Chapter 2: Fields](./02_FIELDS.md), [Chapter 3: Practices](./03_PRACTICES.md)
> 
> **Key Concepts**: Domain, Practice × Field, Accumulation, Barrenness, Defamiliarization
>
> **Learning Objectives**: By the end, you will understand domains as stabilized practice-field pairings, and know how to diagnose and address barrenness.

---

## What a Domain Is

A domain is a **stabilized pairing of a practice and a field**.

```
Domain = Practice × Field
```

When the practice of writing engages the field of narrative prose, and this pairing stabilizes, you get the domain "fiction writing."

When phenomenological attention engages the field of doing laundry, and this pairing stabilizes, you get a domain (perhaps idiosyncratic to you: "phenomenological attention to domestic labor").

---

## Why the Pairing Matters

Domains aren't just labels. They're generative contexts with:

- **Accumulated sites** — positions already found
- **Developed constraints** — restrictions already established
- **Characteristic gestures** — movements that feel natural here
- **Barrenness patterns** — where it feels exhausted vs. fresh

When you enter a domain, you enter a territory with history.

---

## Domain Examples

| Domain | Practice | Field |
|--------|----------|-------|
| Short fiction | Writing | Narrative prose (bounded by length) |
| Literary fiction | Writing | Narrative prose (bounded by conventions) |
| Music theory | Analysis | Musical structures |
| Jazz improvisation | Musical performance | Jazz idiom |
| Phenomenology of perception | Phenomenological attention | Perceptual experience |
| Organizational consulting | Design intervention | Corporate dynamics |

---

## Same Field, Different Domains

When different practices engage the same field, different domains emerge:

| Field | Practice | Domain |
|-------|----------|--------|
| Renaissance painting | Art history | Art historical study of Renaissance |
| Renaissance painting | Studio practice | Painting in Renaissance tradition |
| Renaissance painting | Conservation | Restoration/preservation work |
| Renaissance painting | Forgery | Replication practice |

Each domain has its own accumulated sites and characteristic constraints.

---

## Same Practice, Different Domains

When the same practice engages different fields, different domains emerge:

| Practice | Field | Domain |
|----------|-------|--------|
| Writing | Narrative prose | Fiction |
| Writing | Technical subjects | Technical writing |
| Writing | Self-examination | Journal/memoir |
| Writing | Persuasion | Rhetoric |

The practice carries over; the sites discovered are field-specific.

---

## Domain Accumulation

Over time, domains accumulate:

### Sites Found

Positions discovered through exploration. In "short fiction," accumulated sites include: the unreliable narrator, the epiphanic ending, the compressed timeline, the minimalist dialogue, etc.

### Constraints Developed

Restrictions established through practice. In "short fiction," constraints include: the single-effect premise, the unity of impression, the economical revelation.

### Knowledge Structures

Inherited frameworks, taxonomies, techniques, heuristics. In "short fiction," this includes narrative theory, workshop conventions, genre expectations.

### Barrenness Patterns

Which regions feel exhausted, which feel fresh. In "short fiction," the surprise-twist ending feels exhausted; second-person present tense felt fresh (for a while).

---

## Barrenness

Barrenness is the experience that a domain has nothing left to offer. "There's nothing here." It comes in types:

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Familiarity** | You know it too well | Can't see your own kitchen anymore |
| **Completion** | Feels "finished" | "This has been fully explored" |
| **Categorical** | Labeled with limiting category | "It's just a chore" |
| **Exhaustion** | Effort without returns | Writing the same story again |
| **Status** | Low status blocks perception | "Nothing serious happens in laundry" |

Barrenness is usually a property of your relationship to the domain, not the domain itself.

---

## Defamiliarization

The cure for barrenness is defamiliarization — making the familiar strange.

### For Familiarity Barrenness

| Practice | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Literal estrangement | Describe for an alien |
| Temporal estrangement | Describe what preceded this |
| Scale estrangement | Examine at 10x or 0.1x scale |

### For Completion Barrenness

| Practice | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Counter-history | Find moments when it could have developed differently |
| Incompleteness hunting | What does the "completion" exclude? |
| Future projection | What purposes might arise that current state doesn't serve? |

### For Categorical Barrenness

| Practice | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Category refusal | Engage without the usual category |
| Category theft | Apply categories from distant domains |
| Meta-categorization | Ask why this gets this category |

### For Exhaustion Barrenness

| Practice | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Sense switching | Engage through different sensory modality |
| Pace switching | Engage at radically different speed |
| Stance switching | Engage from different stance |

### For Status Barrenness

| Practice | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Elevation | Treat as if most important domain |
| Dependency tracing | Trace what depends on this domain |
| Expertise seeking | Find someone who treats this as expertise |

---

## Domain Operations

```
EnterDomain(state, domain)    → State with domain active
SwitchPractice(state, new)    → New domain (same field, different practice)
SwitchField(state, new)       → New domain (same practice, different field)
DiagnoseBarrenness(state)     → Barrenness diagnosis
ApplyDefamiliarization(state) → Reduced barrenness, revealed sites
```

---

## Domain Hierarchy

Domains can nest:

```
Writing
  └── Fiction writing
        └── Short fiction
              └── Flash fiction
              └── Literary short fiction
        └── Novel writing
              └── Literary novel
              └── Genre novel
```

Child domains inherit from parents but add specificity. You can operate at any level. Operating at a broader level gives more freedom; operating at a narrower level gives more accumulated resources.

---

## Translation Between Domains

Sometimes a site found in one domain can translate to another:

**Same field, different practices**: "The moment of becoming clean" (analytical practice × laundry) might translate to "the felt shift from soiled to fresh" (phenomenological practice × laundry).

**Same practice, different fields**: "The unreliable narrator" (writing × fiction) might translate to "the unreliable protagonist" (writing × memoir) or "the biased source" (writing × journalism).

Not all sites translate. Some are specific to their practice-field pairing.

---

## Summary

- **Domain = Practice × Field** (stabilized pairing)
- Domains **accumulate** sites, constraints, knowledge, barrenness patterns
- **Barrenness** is the feeling that a domain has nothing left (familiarity, completion, categorical, exhaustion, status)
- **Defamiliarization** makes the familiar strange, restoring generativity
- Domains form **hierarchies** (parent/child)
- Sites can sometimes **translate** between domains

## Exercises

### Warm-up

**4.1 The Boring Domain**

**Position**: You used to love something. Now it's boring. Name the domain.

| Barrenness Type | Test | Example |
|-----------------|------|---------|
| **Familiarity** | You know what will happen | "Video games are all the same" |
| **Completion** | You've mastered the loop | "I've seen every good movie" |
| **Exhaustion** | Effort yields no surprise | "I've tried everything" |

**Question**: Which type applies to your domain? How do you *know*? (Hint: If you can predict the next move, it's Familiarity. If effort doesn't help, it's Exhaustion.)

**4.2 The Practice Switch**

**Position**: Take "Cooking dinner." Switch the practice:

| Practice | What It Reveals |
|----------|-----------------|
| **Phenomenological** | Textures, smells, heat changes |
| **Engineering** | Heat transfer, emulsion stability |
| **Performance Art** | The violence of chopping, the ritual of serving |

**Question**: Which switch breaks the domestic stupor most effectively? (Hint: Performance Art. It makes the ordinary *theatrical*.)

---

### Standard

**4.3 The Barren Meeting**

**Position**: You're in a meeting at work. The domain feels barren.

| Defamiliarization | Technique | Result |
|-------------------|-----------|--------|
| **Literal** | "Animals in suits making mouth-noises" | Reveals primate hierarchy |
| **Temporal** | "This is a 1950s boardroom ritual" | Reveals obsolescence |
| **Status** | Treat the quietest person as the secret CEO | Reveals hidden influence |

**Question**: Did any technique recover sites? What did you find that was invisible before?

**4.4 Site Translation**

**Position**: Two domains feel separate: "Parenting" and "Professional."

| Parenting Site | Translation | Professional Analog |
|----------------|-------------|---------------------|
| "The Tantrum" | Unregulated emotional discharge | "The Reply-All Chain" |

**Transfer Mechanism**: Both demand attention, resist logic, require containment.

**Question**: If you handle a tantrum by "containing and waiting," what if you handled Reply-All the same way?

**4.5 Barrenness Intervention**

**Position**: You're barren in a domain you've practiced for years.

| Operation | Target | Effect |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| **SWITCH PRACTICE** | Keep field; change lens | "Botanical Survey" turns dog-walking into species-cataloging |

**Question**: What practice would be strange enough to defamiliarize but not so strange you can't engage?

---

### Challenge

**4.6 Defensive Barrenness**

**Position**: Sometimes you call something "boring" because you're afraid of what you'd find if you looked.

| Test | Genuine Barrenness | Defensive Barrenness |
|------|--------------------|----------------------|
| **Sensation** | Flat, sleepy | Tight, irritated |
| **Behavior** | You look, but there's nothing | You look away *quickly* |

**Question**: Can you distinguish them in yourself? Name one case where "boring" was actually "terrifying."

**4.7 The Inherited Domain**

**Position**: You inherited a domain (family religion, professional training). The sites and constraints came pre-loaded. Some don't serve you.

| Operation | Effect |
|-----------|--------|
| **Critical Use** | Use the domain's tools *against* its own constraints |

**Example**: "Corporate Professionalism" has a constraint: "Hide emotions." Critical Use: Use the mask to smuggle subversive ideas behind it.

**Question**: What is the "load-bearing assumption" of your inherited domain? What breaks if you negate it?
