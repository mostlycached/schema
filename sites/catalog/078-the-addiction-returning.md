# The Addiction Returning

## The Site

You were clean. Weeks, months, years. And now: the craving is back. Not as memory but as *want*. The body remembers what the mind tried to forget. The substance — alcohol, opioids, nicotine, whatever it was — is calling. Your will, which you thought was strong, reveals itself as tissue-thin. You might use again. You might be about to use again.

**Dimensions**:
```
ontological: process (relapse unfolding)
scale: extended (the craving can last hours, days)
temporal: oscillation (clean → craving → ??? )
perceptual: somatic (body wants it) + psychological (mind fixates)
relational: solitary (you and the want)
agential: losing (agency draining away)
structural: recursive (craving → resist → craving → resist → ?)

// Sanitization-resistant dimensions
cleanliness: soiled (the self feels contaminated by want)
dignity: degraded (you promised, you failed)
redemption: unredeemed (no lesson, just relapse)
meaning: anti-meaning (recovery narrative collapses)
aesthetic: squalid (the desperation, the sweating, the eyes)
```

## The Phenomenology

### The Craving
```
- Intrusive thought (the substance appears in mind, unbidden)
- Body memory (the mouth waters, the veins itch, the lungs want)
- Fixation (can't think about anything else)
- Rationalization (just once, I've been good, I deserve this)
- Time distortion (every minute of not-using feels like an hour)
```

### The War
```
- Willpower (I won't, I won't, I won't)
- Replacement (do something else, distract, substitute)
- Calling someone (if you still have support)
- Bargaining (maybe just a little, maybe tomorrow)
- The slow erosion (will weakening with each wave)
```

### The Potential Relapse
```
- The moment (you are about to use)
- The first use (the body knows exactly what to do)
- The relief (the craving stops — that's why you did it)
- The shame (immediate, following the relief)
- The fall (back to day one, back to nothing)
```

## What's Revealed

When recovery fails:

1. **The body has memory** — it doesn't forget the substance
2. **Willpower has limits** — it can be exhausted
3. **Recovery is not linear** — clean doesn't mean cured
4. **Agency is conditional** — the self can lose control
5. **The craving is stronger** — sometimes, it just is

---

## The Unredeemed

This site specifically resists redemption:
- You were doing well and now you're not
- There's no lesson except: the substance is still there
- The relapse doesn't make you stronger
- It just makes you a user again

What the sanitization-resistant schema must hold: **the failure of the recovery narrative, the re-falling**.

---

## The Cruel Part

The cruelest aspect: **relief**.

When you use, the craving stops. That's why you do it. The substance works. It always works. The problem is everything else.

The sanitized narrative: "just say no, get help, stay strong."
The reality: the substance feels good and makes the pain stop and the craving unbearable.
