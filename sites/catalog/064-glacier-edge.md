# The Glacier's Edge

## The Site

Where the ice meets the not-ice. The terminus of a river of frozen time, moving meters per year. Here you see centuries compressed — ice that fell as snow when your great-grandparents were young. The glacier calves: house-sized chunks falling into the sea with sounds like cannon fire. You're watching deep time in action, slow violence visible at human scale.

**Dimensions**:
```
ontological: process (slow transformation)
scale: human (visible) + deep time (centuries of snow)
temporal: very slow (meters per year) + punctuated (calving events)
perceptual: direct (you can see and hear it)
relational: non-human (ice age remnant)
agential: observer (witnessing)
structural: boundary-only (ice/not-ice interface)
```

## Flux Relation

```
flux_state: slow flux (moving, but glacially)
dissolution_resistance: decreasing (climate change accelerates melt)
hover_potential: high — can witness for long time
emergence_pattern: from ice accumulation exceeding melt
flux_pockets: what calves is flux; the glacier's existence is (decreasingly) structured
```

The glacier is structured flux — frozen motion, slow flow. At the edge, structure (ice) dissolves into flux (water) or catastrophically collapses (calving). The site is the ongoing boundary between solid and liquid, past and present.
