# The Pleasure in Someone's Suffering

## The Site

You see them suffer. And something in you — a flicker, a warmth — enjoys it. Not in heroic revenge against an oppressor. Just: someone suffers, and you feel something like pleasure. Schadenfreude at its darkest. The capacity for cruelty that sits in you, mostly unused but not absent. The part that watches the humiliation and feels good.

**Dimensions**:
```
ontological: relation (you and the suffering other)
scale: momentary (the flash of pleasure)
temporal: instant (it happens fast, then you cover it)
perceptual: direct (you witness, you feel)
relational: dyadic (you watching them)
agential: anyone (this capacity is universal, though denied)
structural: simple (suffering → pleasure, no elaboration)

// Sanitization-resistant dimensions
cleanliness: clean (no physical dirt — moral dirt)
dignity: contemptible (you should not feel this)
redemption: irredeemable (the feeling already happened)
meaning: anti-meaning (contradicts your moral self-image)
aesthetic: craven (morally ugly)
```

## The Phenomenology

### The Witnessing
```
- You see suffering (someone failing, hurting, humiliated)
- The flash (before thought, before judgment — pleasure)
- The recognition (wait, what was that?)
- The cover (no, I don't feel that, I'm a good person)
- The residue (but you did feel it)
```

### Types of Enjoyed Suffering
```
- Competitor's failure (someone who threatened you, now down)
- Enemy's pain (someone who wronged you, getting theirs)
- Stranger's humiliation (random person embarrassed, you enjoy)
- Successful person's fall (they had more, now less)
- The morally righteous getting exposed (they judged you, now they fail)
```

### The Denial
```
- Reframe (I'm just glad justice was served)
- Minimize (it wasn't really pleasure)
- Project (they deserved it)
- Suppress (I won't think about it)
- Perform (outward sympathy, inner satisfaction)
```

| **Evil as Other** | Bad people do that, not me |

| **Quick suppression** | The feeling happens and is immediately buried |
| **Discourse absence** | We don't talk about our own capacity for cruelty |

## What's Revealed

When you admit the feeling:

1. **Cruelty is human** — not alien, not only monstrous
2. **Pleasure in suffering is common** — schadenfreude is universal
3. **The line is thin** — between feeling and acting
4. **Moral self-image is performance** — what you are is more complex
5. **The capacity is always there** — unused is not absent

---

## The Uncomfortable Truth

Schadenfreude research shows:
- It's universal across cultures
- It increases when the sufferer was envied
- It increases when the sufferer was seen as deserving
- It correlates with later aggression

The pleasure is real. It's not a mistake. It's not a bug. It's part of the system.

---

## The Unredeemed

This site cannot be redeemed:
- You felt pleasure at suffering
- The feeling happened before you could stop it
- It revealed something about you
- Calling it "human" doesn't make it good
- It just is what you are, in part

What the sanitization-resistant schema must hold: **the evil in ordinary people, not explained away**.
