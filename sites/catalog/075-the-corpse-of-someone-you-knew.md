# The Corpse of Someone You Knew

## The Site

The face is theirs. The hands are theirs. But they are not there. You look at the body of someone you knew — parent, friend, lover — and see the limit of personhood. This was a someone. Now it's a something. The flesh remains but the person has evacuated. The corpse is the most abject object: a me that is no longer me, a you that is no longer you.

**Dimensions**:
```
ontological: absence (the person is gone) + substance (the body remains)
scale: trans-momentary (the viewing, the memory)
temporal: instant (death is a threshold) + duration (the body persists, then doesn't)
perceptual: direct (you're looking at it)
relational: dyadic (you and... it? them?)
agential: none (the corpse has no agency)
structural: paradoxical (is/isn't, them/not-them)

// Sanitization-resistant dimensions
cleanliness: filthy → putrid (decay begins immediately)
dignity: contemptible (the body has no dignity once vacant)
redemption: irredeemable (this is not fixable)
meaning: anti-meaning (meaning collapses here)
aesthetic: abject (Kristeva's primary example)
```

## Kristeva's Abject

The corpse is Kristeva's paradigm of the abject:

> "The corpse, seen without God and outside of science, is the utmost of abjection. It is death infecting life."

What makes it abject:
1. **It was me** — was-subject, now object
2. **It threatens identity** — I will be this
3. **It pollutes** — decay spreads, contamination
4. **It cannot be integrated** — neither living nor gone
5. **It produces nausea** — the body recoils from its future

---

## What's Revealed

At the corpse:

1. **Personhood is not body** — the body is here, person is not
2. **The body is meat** — tissue, bone, beginning to rot
3. **Death is not metaphor** — it's this, here, real
4. **Decay is immediate** — the corpse is already decomposing
5. **You are next** — this is your future

---

## The Unredeemed

This site cannot be redeemed:
- The person is not coming back
- The body will rot
- There is no meaning that makes this okay
- The corpse just is

What mourning tries to do: move away from the corpse to memory, narrative, meaning. The corpse itself resists all of that. It just lies there, being dead.
